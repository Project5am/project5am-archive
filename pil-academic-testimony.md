# pil-academic-testimony

Two academic papers presented during the fifteen-month appeal period following an institutional confrontation in the final year of a BA Fine Art degree. Both presented while the appeal was still active. Both documenting the case in real time, at major UK academic institutions, while the institutional machinery was still engaged.

This is PIL operating under maximum load. The chaos of the period becoming structured public argument simultaneously with the production of Flux Without Pause. Kairos held inside Chronos.

---

## The Incident

A prominent artist, invited to speak at the institution, made offensive comments about slavery during a critique of *12 Years A Slave*. The comments were received with laughter by the audience.

Jason was the only Black student in the room — and also working at the event. Present in a dual capacity that made exit or non-response structurally more complicated.

The institution's response: freedom of speech. The artist's comments isolated from the crowd's response. The crowd's laughter — the collective institutional response in the room — removed from the official account. The structural concern repositioned as a matter of individual speech. The person raising it repositioned as the problem.

The deflection separated the comment from its reception. In doing so it made the most significant part of the event — that an audience laughed — institutionally invisible. The laughter was the culture. The artist's comment was the trigger. The institution defended the trigger and erased the response.

## Talk One — SOAS, University of London

**November 2014**

*Intersectionality: A Space for Theoretical and Practitioner Discussion*

Presented at the School of Oriental and African Studies, University of London.

Abstract:

*"In the shadow of white privilege... making the invisible visible. A case study of the University of Suffolk's handling of racism/discrimination. The title of this presentation stems from the challenges that people of non-Caucasian backgrounds have to face in a Caucasian dominated system. The reason why I have created this presentation is because despite UCS showing they do not understand the true notion of diversity, they will use my cultural background via statistics and images to promote to potential customers a surface level of diversity, which is a weak notion of diversity."*

**The PIL reading**

The surface diversity critique is the alignment axis stated in academic language in November 2014. The gap between stated institutional identity — commitment to equality and diversity — and preponderance — actual campus culture, hiring patterns, course materials, website images, event design — named precisely and publicly at one of the UK's most significant academic institutions.

The phrase *surface level of diversity* is the structural finding. Not that the institution was hostile to diversity. That it had developed a functional relationship with diversity as image — the statistics, the website photographs, the prospectus — that substituted for diversity as culture. The surface served the function without requiring the substance. This is schema fit failure named correctly: the form present, the living pattern beneath it absent.

The title *making the invisible visible* is the PIL perceptual prerequisite stated as an academic and political project. The pattern is speaking. The institution cannot hear it because hearing it would require acknowledging the gap between its stated identity and its actual preponderance. The talk makes the gap visible at SOAS while the institution is still processing the appeal.

## Talk Two — CERES Conference, University of Edinburgh

**June 2015**

*Invisible Visible: A case study of one university's handling of racism/discrimination*
Jason Haye, University Campus X

Abstract:

*"The challenges that people of non-Caucasian backgrounds have to face in a Eurocentric dominated Education system. University Campus X (UCX) is an educational institution located in an English county. UCX accepted its first students in September 2007. UCX hosted a talk by a prominent artist who during the event made offensive comments about slavery during her critique of the film 12 Years A Slave which was received with laughter by the audience. I was the only Black student in the room who was also working at the event. During the past 8 months, despite trying to challenge the university to look at this incident from a minority student point of view via artwork and debates, UCX has avoided responsibility in tackling this issue by hiding behind themes of freedom of speech and isolating the artist's comments from the crowd's response. The result of this incident has seen my projected overall grade go down. The reason why I have created this paper is because despite UCX showing they do not understand the true notion of diversity, UCX will use Black and minority ethnic students' cultural background via statistics and images to promote to potential customers a surface level and false notion of diversity."*

**The PIL reading**

The Edinburgh abstract does something the SOAS talk could not do from inside the immediate aftermath. It states the complete structural argument with academic precision.

*"hiding behind themes of freedom of speech and isolating the artist's comments from the crowd's response"*

This is the deflection with evidence move named in academic language eight years before PIL would have a name for it. The institution found something defensible — the artist's right to speak — and used it to make the most significant part of the event disappear. The crowd's laughter was the institutional culture revealing itself. The freedom of speech defence made the laughter invisible and positioned the individual's speech as the only thing requiring evaluation.

The crowd's response is named explicitly as part of the incident. This is the structural integrity reading: the event cannot be understood by examining the artist's comment alone. The comment and the reception are one event. Separating them is the move. Naming them together is the correction.

The grade impact is stated plainly. The institution's handling of the appeal affected the final results. The deflection had material consequences for the academic path of the person who raised the original concern. This is recorded in an academic paper presented at the University of Edinburgh's CERES Conference while the appeal was still running.

## The Broader Argument

The Edinburgh abstract frames the case inside a structural analysis of Eurocentric dominated education systems — not as an individual incident but as a case study of a systemic pattern. A single institution, a single incident, used to illuminate the structural condition that produced it.

This is PIL's generative application stated as academic method. The particular case used to reveal the general pattern. The institution not as a villain but as a site where the structural forces operating across the entire system became visible in a specific moment.

The surface diversity critique — institutions using Black and minority ethnic students' cultural background via statistics and images to promote a false notion of diversity to potential customers — is named as a structural practice across Eurocentric dominated education systems, not as the behaviour of a single bad institution.

That framing, made at Edinburgh in 2015, is more structurally precise than most institutional EDI frameworks operating today.
