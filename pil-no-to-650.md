# pil-no-to-650

The origin document. Written and published while the incident was still active, in Jason's own voice, making the structural argument to peers and the public simultaneously. 509 people signed it.

Everything that followed — the SOAS talk, the Edinburgh paper, the external inquiry, the Provost's apology, Flux Without Pause, the fifteen-month appeal, the academic presentations, PIL itself — follows from this document. This is where the argument was first made publicly, in full, under maximum pressure, with nothing to protect and everything at stake.

Source: ipetitions.com/petition/no-to-650

## The Petition

*"I have created this petition because over the past month my university experience has been altered since the Maggie Hambling talk where the artist made an inappropriate comment about slavery which the majority of the audience that attended greeted it with a dreadful 1970s styled laughter. This was a humiliating experience for me as I was the only black person there and also the only person there working filming the event. I wasn't the only person offended since there has been several letters of complaint.*

*What has saddened me over the past weeks has been the lack of action taken over this serious issue by the course leader. It was this lack of action and awareness that has forced me to create a series of videos that captured what it feels like to be alienated in this kind of environment.*

*The last four weeks has shown me that the subject matter has not been addressed because of the commodity that the artist in question brings to the university and because the artist had work at the auction the issue was attempted to be swept under the carpet.*

*Examples:*

*— Setting up a hierarchical framework for the talk which gave an over importance to the artist e.g. sending emails asking for intelligent questions limiting the space to challenge the artist's opinions.*

*— Not sending a simple apology to the group for her remarks that would have showed that he was aware of what had taken place.*

*— Several letters of complaint referred to as people "jumping on the band wagon".*

*— Censoring my work by mentioning legal action and saying I needed permission and calling it a private talk when there were members of the public in attendance.*

*— Advising the fine art student rep not to mention the incident at the next committee meeting.*

*This list that I made captures positivist thought, which operates in most western universities around the world.*

*Positivist thought: "Divorces the fact from its social and historical context." "Under these conditions the interdependence of knowledge, imagination, will and creativity are lost in a reduction of all phenomena to the rule of the empirical."*

*Positivist thought does not allow students to question their surroundings compared to critical pedagogy, a framework that is more focused on problem solving dialogue on a level setting for all positions participating.*

*It is for those reasons why I feel that the money from the Hambling painting should not be associated with the degree show as it for me represents what is wrong with a university which follows this way of thinking. It creates selfish individuals, it puts commodity over student wellbeing and it teaches students that this is the way to be, which is frightening because a lot of students will go on to become teachers so that this cycle of behaviour will continue.*

*Some people might be thinking that this doesn't affect them because they were not in attendance or that they are not black so it is not really their problem, but if you look at it from the point of view that someone on your team, from no part of his own making, has been alienated from a group that has grown together over the past three years because of maintaining an image of an artist in order of selling a painting at the auction for just £650... is that worth it?*

*I feel that by making this stand as a group, it would make a tremendous statement that a university setting should stand for equality and that students are not robots and they have the right to challenge frameworks that are wrong without having to feel isolated in the process.*

*There is an ethical weight to this money that hasn't been mentioned before and I'll leave it up to you to make your choice.*

*On my part I have done all I could to highlight how bad this is — for me personally I find it untenable to take part in the show with this money included because I don't want the next student having to carry the same weight that I have because it is not a good place to be.*

*But this experience has also been a gift because coming across critical pedagogy has given me language that can match my project with and finding a language for my project was the reason for going to university.*

*Paulo Freire says, in the Pedagogy of the Oppressed, that education either functions as an instrument which is used to facilitate integration of the younger generation into the logic of the present system and bring about conformity or it becomes the practice of freedom, the means by which men and women deal critically and creatively with reality and discover how to participate in the transformation of their world.*

*Kind regards, Jason Haye"*

## The PIL Reading

**The institutional moves listed in real time**

The petition names five specific institutional moves while they are still happening:

The hierarchical framework pre-limiting space to challenge the artist — structural integrity failure at the level of event design. The space was engineered to protect the speaker before she arrived.

Letters of complaint dismissed as jumping on the bandwagon — delegitimisation of multiple concerns by grouping them as collective overreaction rather than reading each one on its structural merits.

Legal threats used to censor artwork documenting the experience — the practice of freedom meeting the institution's commodity interest. The censorship attempt confirms the petition's central argument: the artist's commercial relationship with the institution was the operative value, not student wellbeing.

The fine art student rep advised not to mention the incident at the next committee meeting — institutional governance actively suppressing the concern from reaching the structures designed to address it. The channel closed before the message could travel through it.

Calling it a private talk when members of the public were in attendance — factual misrepresentation deployed to narrow the definition of accountability.

These are not interpretations. They are documented actions, listed in sequence, by the person they were directed at, while they were happening. The PIL drift detection axis doesn't require retrospective analysis here. The drift is visible in real time, in a public document, signed by 509 people who recognised the pattern.

**The positivist thought critique**

*"Divorces the fact from its social and historical context."*

This is the schema fit failure named in theoretical language in a student petition, in 2014, before the academic papers, before PIL. Isolating the artist's comment from the crowd's response is positivist thought in operation — the laughter separated from the comment, the structural condition made invisible, the fact stripped of its historical weight.

Freire and Giroux cited alongside the institutional behaviour. The theoretical framework placed around the moves while the moves were still being made. Critical pedagogy not as academic reference but as survival tool — the language that made the structural argument possible under pressure.

**The £650**

The painting at auction. £650. The commercial relationship that made the incident structurally impossible for the institution to address honestly.

*"Is that worth it?"*

The ethical weight of the money named plainly. Not as hyperbole. As a structural fact: the institution's handling of the incident was shaped by the commodity value of the artist's association. The student's wellbeing was weighed against £650 and found to be worth less. The petition names this without drama and asks peers to make their own choice.

509 people said no.

**The gift**

*"But this experience has also been a gift because coming across critical pedagogy has given me language that can match my project with and finding a language for my project was the reason for going to university."*

This line is the PIL origin stated at the moment of maximum pressure. The institution failing in its stated educational function while accidentally providing the theoretical framework that made the practice legible to itself. Freire arriving through necessity rather than curriculum. The language found not through instruction but through the need to describe an irresolvable experience.

The gift is real. PIL is one of its products. The petition is the first document where this is stated — not in retrospect, not from a position of safety, but in the middle of it, while the appeal was still to come, while the grade impact had not yet been fully felt, while the institution was still engaged.

The experience as gift is the anti-calcification principle operating under conditions that would have destroyed a less structurally sound practice. The hollow being cleaned, not by choice, by pressure.

**The Freire closing**

Education as conformity or education as the practice of freedom.

The petition chose freedom. Not as a rhetorical position. As a practical decision — the £650 is ethically compromised, the show cannot include it, here is why, sign if you agree.

509 people agreed. The practice of freedom had a constituency.

## What This Document Is in the Toolkit

Every other PIL document was written after the pressure had passed or was being processed at academic distance. This one was written inside it.

The petition is the practice under maximum load producing its clearest argument. No distance. No retrospective framing. No theoretical scaffolding beyond what was immediately necessary. The structural moves named as they happened. The ethical argument made plainly. The community invited to decide.

PIL's claim is that genuine knowing is produced by experience, pressure, and convergence — not instruction. This document is that claim demonstrated in the moment of its own production. The framework that would later name what was happening was being built, in public, in real time, by someone who had not yet found the full language for it but was already using it.

Finding a language for the project was the reason for going to university. The institution provided the experience that made the language necessary. PIL is that language, fully developed.

The petition is where it started.
