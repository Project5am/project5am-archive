# pil-rsm-correspondence

An email exchange from October 14, 2010. Three things in a single document: the Project 5am axiom in its first written form, the resonance field producing an unscripted arrival, and external recognition from a practitioner who had spent decades doing what Project 5am was just beginning to articulate.

---

## The Exchange

**From R. Stevie Moore to Jason Haye — arriving first:**

*"Thanks for checking out my project, means a lot to me.*

*I found you by following my bliss ;-)*

*It started last night from downloading a Slapp Happy album. That led me to reading an interview with a guy who mentioned that one of his favourite albums is David Crosby's 'If I Could Only Remember My Name'. That led me to find out about the top ten all time albums selected by the Vatican which also included 'The Nightfly' by Donald Fagen. The same interview also talked warmly about Virginia Astley. Her name sounded familiar so I checked out her song and then I heard 'A Summer Long Since Passed' which blew me away because I had heard that track on the Michael Andrews soundtrack for the film Me You and Everybody We Know which is one of my favourite films of my life. I saw the soundtrack listing on CD Baby which had a sidebar for other recommended albums if you liked the soundtrack to Me You and Everybody We Know. So I followed the link and I found some great 80s French minimal beats like Deux - Game & Performance and a few YouTube links later I found a wonderful track by Solid Space called 10th Planet. I searched for some information about the band then I found a blog site which had an mp3 mix that featured Solid Space, Ariel Pink and... your good self sir.*

*The track was 'Get The Point' but the first link I found was 'Too Old (To Fall in Love)' and boom, that led me to finding you. You have an amazing discography, I look forward to hearing more of your work including your recent electronic sounds.*

*So I found you by not looking for you... that's the beauty of music.*

*Stay yellow ;-)*"

## Three Things This Document Confirms

**The axiom in its first written form**

Jason's email to R. Stevie Moore contains the Project 5am axiom — sent to another practitioner, October 2010, before any framework existed to contextualise it. This is the axiom's first documented appearance as text given to another person. Not published, not performed. Sent directly as an introduction — the thing that explains what the practice is before anything else is said.

The sequencing matters. The axiom comes first. Before the website link. Before the Archive.org link. Before the description. The condition is created before the content is named. That is PIL's generative application stated as instinct in 2010 — three years before Flux Without Pause, eight years before Surf/ace, fifteen years before PIL had a name for what it was doing.

**The resonance field producing an unscripted arrival**

The chain R. Stevie Moore describes: Slapp Happy → David Crosby → Vatican top ten → Donald Fagen → Virginia Astley → Michael Andrews → *Me You and Everybody We Know* → CD Baby sidebar → Deux → Solid Space → Ariel Pink → Project 5am.

*"I found you by not looking for you — that's the beauty of music."*

This is the resonance field operating in real time, documented in an email, in 2010. Someone moving through genuine listening — following what resonates, not searching for anything specific — arriving at Project 5am through the territory the practice was already inhabiting. The field drew him toward it without instruction.

The Miranda July circuit is the structural centre of the chain. *Me You and Everybody We Know* is the film whose yellow principle had already been adopted into the practice — the thing that makes everything come alive after you've given up. R. Stevie Moore arrives at Project 5am through that film's soundtrack. He ends his email with *stay yellow* — the principle reflected back, having travelled through a chain of listening that passed through the same film on the way here.

Yellow completed a circuit it didn't know it was part of.

**External recognition from a practitioner who understood**

R. Stevie Moore is the godfather of lo-fi. DIY recording pioneer since the 1970s. Someone who built a practice entirely outside institutional structures before there was a vocabulary for doing so — before independent, before lo-fi, before any of the frameworks that would later describe what he was doing had been articulated. His entire career is a demonstration of preponderance over instruction: the accumulated practice of decades producing work on his own terms, in his own home, distributed through whatever channels were available.

*"nice stuff, like a lot"*

That is his complete review. From someone who had heard everything. It is enough.

His immediate question — how to download the whole album — is the response of a listener who wants to keep going. Not a courtesy. A request.

## Who R. Stevie Moore Is

Robert Steven Moore, born 1952, New Jersey. Began home recording in the early 1970s. Released hundreds of albums independently across five decades, distributed through mail order and later digital platforms, before any institutional framework existed for doing so. Credited as a foundational influence on lo-fi, home recording, and DIY music culture. Described by critics as the godfather of lo-fi.

His practice is the longest-running demonstration in recorded music history of a practitioner building entirely from preponderance — accumulated pattern authority developed through continuous making, outside institutional validation, over fifty years.

*"how did u find me?"* — his question back to Jason. The field worked in both directions. Two practitioners who had never encountered each other, moving through the same territory, finding each other through the work.
