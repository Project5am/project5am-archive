# pil-blog-archive

Two blog entries from the Flux Without Pause archive. The first predates the incident. The second is the immediate, unprocessed response to it — written before the petition, before the SOAS talk, before the appeal, before everything.

Read together they establish something the rest of the archive cannot: the axiom existed before the pressure that supposedly produced the framework that carries it. The pattern was already running. The incident made it fully visible.

---

## Entry One — October 25, 2013

Posted under: Flux Without Pause

*"Infinite metamorphosis, formulas of forms. Bred by taste of the act in the incomprehensible roams."*

---

This is the Flux Without Pause axiom. Posted four months before the incident. Before the petition. Before the SOAS talk. Before the fifteen-month appeal. Before the album. Before PIL.

The framework named itself before the experience that supposedly produced it.

The PIL revision this forces: Flux Without Pause was not produced by the 2014 institutional confrontation. The confrontation made the axiom's meaning fully visible — it supplied the conditions under which the pattern already running could be seen clearly. But the pattern was there in October 2013, posted quietly on a blog, untitled, under the heading that would become the framework's name.

This is the deepest confirmation of PIL's core claim. Genuine knowing operates before and beneath the events that make it legible. The axiom preceded the pressure. The pressure did not produce the knowing. It revealed that the knowing was already there.

## Entry Two — March 6, 2014

Posted under: Flux Without Pause

*"I made a new invisible man video in order to own the experience given to me after attending and helping out at a Maggi Hambling talk where some very inappropriate comments were made about slavery. In my previous post I described what it was like to be there and also how I was let down by audience reaction to her comments as they have been my peers for the past three years.*

*Of course this is not referred to everybody as a few of my fellow students have shared their disgust over the incident but only one person has come up to me to share some empathy and disgust without myself having to bring up the issue.*

*This leads me on to why I didn't walk out after hearing those comments. First I was in shock for a few minutes and then I thought from past experience where I have been stopped and searched and I have responded with anger which makes you lose your focus and kind of live up to certain stereotypes. So I sat through the end of the talk and waited for the response from others. In the very few discussions I have had I have been asked how does slavery affect myself and my family — I really thought this was obvious and I am not going to bother explaining my family history. What I can do is describe via my art what it feels like to be alienated in a room so I have created this piece so people can feel that vibe and at the same time see the power of being invisible as captured in Ralph Ellison's Invisible Man via the French critical theorist Jacques Rancière's notions of using shock to raise awareness of frameworks.*

*While planning to make this piece I found out that designer Eddie Opara also used Ellison's book for his project stealth.*

*'I remember reading Ralph Ellison's Invisible Man, when I was younger, and the aspects of identity, especially being black. And also, the idea of being African American in America... This is: I am invisible. Understand simply because people refuse to see me.'*

*Apart from making this video to own my experience I have also made it to show people the example of Russian theorist Mikhail Bakhtin's term carnivalesque in full effect where Bakhtin explains the temporal space of a carnival as a space to do things that you wouldn't normally do. I very much imagine that if anyone else had said those comments it would have been treated with a different response but because it was someone famous and very transgressive in their actions people went along with the ride.*

*It was just the whole scenario about that topic put me in an unnecessary paradoxical situation.*

*Of course there's the invisibility aspect of those who responded to those subject matter in that crass manner when the only person working at the talk was a black guy and it just dawned on me today that there were other people in the audience who were just as uncomfortable with the remarks but were looking at me to make a stand by walking out or speaking out... that's a lot of weight to carry when I'm just a student who came for a talk about art.*

*So making this work and getting it out there will hopefully avoid that weight being passed on to future students."*

---

## The PIL Reading

**The decision not to walk out**

*"From past experience where I have been stopped and searched and I have responded with anger which makes you lose your focus and kind of live up to certain stereotypes."*

The calculation made in the room, in real time, under shock. Not passivity. Structural awareness operating under acute pressure. The perceptual capacity to read the room's expectations, anticipate how a response would be received and reframed, and navigate consciously while still inside the experience.

This is double consciousness as a real-time survival skill — the capacity to see yourself being seen, to read the structural expectations being placed on you, and to move through them without being destroyed by them. Du Bois named it. Ellison lived it. The blog post documents it happening in a fine art lecture theatre in 2014.

The PIL resonance axis is usually described as the capacity to register when a pattern is speaking before you can explain why. This entry shows its other face: the capacity to read how your own response will be patterned by others before you make it. Both directions of the same perceptual capacity.

**The artwork as ownership**

*"I made this piece so people can feel that vibe and at the same time see the power of being invisible."*

Not explanation. Not argument. Ownership. The practice converting an irresolvable experience into form immediately — within weeks of the incident, before the petition, before any institutional process had begun. The Invisible Man video as the first PIL output of the 2014 period: the experience made into work, the work made public, the structural condition given a form that others could encounter rather than just a description they could agree or disagree with.

Rancière's use of shock to raise awareness of frameworks — the artwork designed to produce an encounter that bypasses the explanatory loop. Not: here is what happened and why it was wrong. But: here is what it felt like, encounter it directly. This is PIL's generative application as artistic instinct, operating in March 2014.

**Bakhtin's carnivalesque**

*"Because it was someone famous and very transgressive in their actions people went along with the ride."*

The structural reading placed around the audience's laughter within weeks of the incident. The carnivalesque names the condition: the temporary suspension of normal social rules inside a space licensed by the presence of a transgressive authority. The laughter was not individual moral failure — it was a structural condition produced by the event's design. The hierarchical framework pre-limiting space to challenge the artist, named in the petition, is the carnivalesque architecture made visible.

The institution created the conditions for the laughter and then isolated the comment from the response. Bakhtin in the blog. Rancière in the artwork. Freire in the petition. The theoretical framework assembled from necessity in real time, each piece arriving when it was needed.

**The weight**

*"That's a lot of weight to carry when I'm just a student who came for a talk about art."*

The clearest statement of what the confrontation actually was. Not a political debate. Not a confrontation between equals. A student who came for a talk about art, found himself the only Black person in the room, working at the event, bearing the weight of an entire room's expectation that he respond on behalf of a history he hadn't chosen to represent in that moment.

The weight is structural. It was placed there by the room's architecture before the artist spoke. The only Black person present, also working, also being filmed — positioned before the incident occurred to carry whatever happened next. The incident didn't create the weight. It revealed that the weight was already there and had been there for three years.

**The motivation**

*"So making this work and getting it out there will hopefully avoid that weight being passed on to future students."*

This line closes the March 2014 blog post, the petition, the SOAS talk, the Edinburgh paper, and PIL itself. The same motivation throughout, stated first here, in a blog post written weeks after the incident.

Not vindication. Not argument won. Not institution changed. The next student not having to carry it alone. The work made so the weight becomes visible before it lands on someone who doesn't know it's coming.

PIL is that work, fully developed. The blog post is where the motivation was first stated.

## The Axiom Preceding the Incident

The October 2013 entry requires one further reading.

The Flux Without Pause axiom posted in October 2013 is not identical to the final form. *"Bred by taste of the act in the incomprehensible roams"* — the act, not improvising. The incomprehensible, not the ineffable. The movement of language between October 2013 and the final axiom is itself a PIL trace — the pattern authority accumulating, the language finding its most precise form through continued practice.

The axiom in its first form. The incident four months later. The framework named. The language refined. The toolkit built. The archive read backwards to this entry.

The pattern was running in October 2013. Everything else is the process of making it visible.
